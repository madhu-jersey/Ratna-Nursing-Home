General Medicine

Diagnosis and care for everyday and long-term illnesses, from a fever to diabetes, thyroid and blood pressure.

Common problems

What patients notice

Common reasons people come to this department.

Draft, pending doctor review

  • Fever

    A high temperature, body pains, chills or tiredness, sometimes with a cough or loose motions.

  • Diabetes (high sugar)

    Thirst, passing urine often, weight loss, tiredness, or wounds that heal slowly. Often there are no signs at all.

  • Thyroid problems

    Weight change, tiredness, feeling too hot or too cold, hair fall, irregular periods or a swelling in the neck.

  • High blood pressure

    Usually no symptoms. Sometimes headaches, giddiness or blurred vision.

  • Breathing problems

    A cough that will not settle, wheezing, breathlessness, or chest tightness.

  • Kidney-related problems

    Swelling of the feet or face, burning or frequent urination, or changes found in a blood test.

When to get help straight away

Our emergency department is open 24 hours, every day. Call 08819 222250.

Draft, pending doctor review

  1. Chest pain or pressure, especially with sweating or breathlessness: call 108 or go to the nearest emergency department at once.
  2. Sudden weakness or numbness of the face, arm or leg, or slurred speech: these can be signs of a stroke. Call 108 immediately.
  3. Severe breathlessness, a fit, or a person who is very drowsy or confused: call 108.
  4. Very high or very low sugar readings with drowsiness or confusion: get medical help straight away.
  5. A high fever with a rash, bleeding gums, severe headache or stiff neck: come in the same day.

Why they happen

A short explanation for each problem above.

Draft, pending doctor review

Fever
Most fevers are the body fighting an infection, such as a viral illness, typhoid, malaria, dengue or a urine infection. A fever that lasts more than three days needs tests to find the cause.
Diabetes (high sugar)
The body cannot use sugar properly, because it makes too little insulin or does not respond to it. Over the years, high sugar can damage the eyes, kidneys, nerves and heart.
Thyroid problems
The thyroid gland in the neck sets the body's pace. When it makes too little or too much hormone, many parts of the body slow down or speed up.
High blood pressure
Pressure in the blood vessels stays higher than it should. Left untreated, it strains the heart, brain and kidneys, which is why regular checks matter.
Breathing problems
Infections such as bronchitis and pneumonia, asthma, allergies and long-term smoking all narrow or inflame the airways.
Kidney-related problems
Urine infections, stones, and long-standing diabetes or blood pressure can affect how well the kidneys filter the blood.

Common causes

Things that make these problems more likely.

Draft, pending doctor review

  • Infections from food, water and mosquitoes
  • Family history of diabetes or blood pressure
  • Little exercise and weight gain
  • Too much salt, sugar and oily food
  • Smoking and alcohol
  • Stress and poor sleep
  • Missed check-ups and medicines

Solutions

Treatment options

What each option involves and what recovery usually looks like. Your doctor will advise which suits you.

Draft, pending doctor review

  • Consultation and check-up

    What it involves

    A full history, examination, and checks of blood pressure, pulse, temperature and weight.

    Recovery

    No recovery needed.

  • Tests

    What it involves

    Blood and urine tests, an ECG, and a scan where needed, to find the cause and plan treatment.

    Recovery

    Results are explained at a review visit.

    Tests done on site to be confirmed

  • Long-term care for sugar, thyroid and BP

    What it involves

    Medicines, diet and activity advice, and a schedule of review visits and tests to keep the condition controlled.

    Recovery

    An ongoing plan that is adjusted at each visit.

  • Inpatient care

    What it involves

    Admission to the ward for illnesses that need fluids, injections, oxygen or close watching.

    Recovery

    Discharge once the doctor is satisfied, with a written summary and medicines.

    Ward facilities to be confirmed

What to expect

Three stages, from your first visit to aftercare.

Draft, pending doctor review

  1. Consultation

    The doctor asks about your symptoms, medicines and family history, examines you, and suggests tests if needed.

  2. Procedure

    Treatment starts as an outpatient where possible. If you need closer care, the doctor will explain why and arrange admission.

  3. Follow-up

    You leave with written advice and medicines, and a review date for long-term conditions.
