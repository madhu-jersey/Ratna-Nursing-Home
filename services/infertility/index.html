Common problems

What patients notice

Common reasons people come to this department.

Draft, pending doctor review

  • Not conceiving

    A year of trying without pregnancy, or six months if the woman is over 35.

  • Irregular ovulation

    Irregular or absent periods.

  • PCOD

    Irregular periods, weight gain or acne along with difficulty conceiving.

  • Tube and uterus problems

    Painful periods, past pelvic infections, or earlier surgery.

  • Age and repeated loss

    Trying later in life, or more than one miscarriage.

Why they happen

A short explanation for each problem above.

Draft, pending doctor review

Not conceiving
Conception needs a healthy egg, healthy sperm, open tubes and a receptive uterus. A problem with any of these, in either partner, can delay pregnancy.
Irregular ovulation
If eggs are not released regularly, there are fewer chances each year to conceive. PCOD and thyroid problems are common causes.
PCOD
Hormone imbalance stops eggs from maturing and being released on time.
Tube and uterus problems
Blocked tubes, fibroids, polyps or endometriosis can stop the egg and sperm from meeting, or the embryo from settling.
Age and repeated loss
Egg numbers and quality fall with age, especially after 35. Repeated loss needs tests for both partners.

Common causes

Things that make these problems more likely.

Draft, pending doctor review

  • PCOD and hormone imbalance
  • Thyroid problems
  • Blocked fallopian tubes
  • Fibroids and endometriosis
  • Low sperm count or movement
  • Age
  • Weight, smoking and alcohol

Solutions

Treatment options

What each option involves and what recovery usually looks like. Your doctor will advise which suits you.

Draft, pending doctor review

  • Evaluation of both partners

    What it involves

    History, examination, blood tests for hormones, a pelvic scan for the woman, and a semen test for the man.

    Recovery

    No recovery needed. Results are explained together.

  • Ovulation induction

    What it involves

    Tablets or injections help eggs grow and be released, with scans to time intercourse.

    Recovery

    A few visits in each treatment cycle.

  • Follicular monitoring

    What it involves

    Repeat scans across the cycle to see when an egg is ready.

    Recovery

    Short visits over one to two weeks.

  • IUI (intrauterine insemination)

    What it involves

    Prepared sperm is placed in the uterus at the time of ovulation, through a thin tube.

    Recovery

    Usual activities the same day.

    Whether IUI is offered to be confirmed

  • Laparoscopy for fertility

    What it involves

    Keyhole surgery to check the tubes and treat cysts, endometriosis or adhesions.

    Recovery

    Usually home within a day or two.

    To be confirmed by doctor

What to expect

Three stages, from your first visit to aftercare.

Draft, pending doctor review

  1. Consultation

    Both partners meet the doctor together. The doctor asks about your history and plans the first tests.

  2. Procedure

    Tests are done over one or two cycles. The doctor then explains the cause, if one is found, and the treatment options.

  3. Follow-up

    Treatment goes ahead cycle by cycle, with scans and reviews, and the plan is adjusted as needed.
