Common problems

Why a scan is needed

The usual reasons a doctor asks for an ultrasound.

Draft, pending doctor review

  • Scans in pregnancy

    Your doctor asks for a scan to check the pregnancy at a certain stage.

  • Pain in the abdomen

    Pain in the upper or lower abdomen, bloating, or vomiting.

  • Period and fertility problems

    Irregular or heavy periods, or difficulty getting pregnant.

  • Follow-up of a known condition

    Your doctor wants to see whether a cyst, stone or other finding has changed.

Why they happen

A short explanation for each problem above.

Draft, pending doctor review

Scans in pregnancy
Scans confirm the pregnancy and due date, check the baby's growth, position and the placenta, and look for problems early.
Pain in the abdomen
A scan can show gallstones, kidney stones, an inflamed appendix, or problems of the liver, ovaries or uterus.
Period and fertility problems
A pelvic scan shows the uterus and ovaries, including fibroids, cysts, PCOD, and whether eggs are growing and being released.
Follow-up of a known condition
Repeat scans show whether treatment is working or a condition is changing.

Things that make these problems more likely.

Solutions

Treatment options

What each option involves and what recovery usually looks like. Your doctor will advise which suits you.

Draft, pending doctor review

  • Obstetric (pregnancy) scan

    What it involves

    A scan over the abdomen, or sometimes internally in early pregnancy, to check the baby and the pregnancy.

    Recovery

    No recovery needed.

  • Abdominal scan

    What it involves

    A probe with gel is moved over the abdomen to look at the liver, gall bladder, kidneys and other organs.

    Recovery

    No recovery needed.

  • Pelvic scan

    What it involves

    A scan of the uterus and ovaries, over the abdomen or internally, depending on what the doctor needs to see.

    Recovery

    No recovery needed.

  • Follicular monitoring

    What it involves

    A series of scans over a cycle to watch eggs grow and be released, for couples trying to conceive.

    Recovery

    Several short visits during one cycle.

What to expect

Three stages, from your first visit to aftercare.

Draft, pending doctor review

  1. Consultation

    Your doctor explains why the scan is needed and how to prepare, for example with a full bladder or after fasting.

  2. Procedure

    The scan takes a few minutes. Gel is applied and a probe is moved over the skin. It does not use radiation.

  3. Follow-up

    The doctor explains the findings and what happens next, and you receive a written report.
